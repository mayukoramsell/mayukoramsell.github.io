GLitch Art:Rotting



"Rotting" is a series of 3 different glitch art of flowers of varying digital sizes manipulated and changed via notepad++ and audacity. The flowers are digitally manipulated and show sections of the images gone, altered in color, or repeated.
The subjects of this work are flowers as I thought their representation of death was similar yet opposite to glitch art. I thought they were similar in the terms that digital media is not always permanent. If something crashes, is unsaved, saved to only one device or uncared for overtime leading to code rot, the piece of media is gone. However, glitch art is also the opposite to death, as it gives digital media new life by creating something new out of something that already exists. I chose 3 different images of flowers from the Bing search engine to explore how different image sizes are affected by the inclusion or exclusion of data when manipulated in notepad++. I did this a few times to each image until it created an image I was satisfied with. When the largest file proved to be difficult to manipulate via notepad++, I put the file through audacity and created this new image.In notepad++, I deleted various lines, added various texts, and copied and pasted text to change how these images appeared. For audacity, I input the image file, put an effect on the audio file and placed the file into photoshop to produce the final result. I did have some trial and errors from accidentally deleting copies to having to find ways to prevent gray blocks from appearing. However, in the end the images created were satisfactory.
I found the process of creating glitch art interesting, as I had to go through various trials to become satisfied with the final result. I purposely created a piece that is a series as I wanted to explore how the concept of glitch/rotting is not exclusive to one thing, or rather is something that can happen to anyone/anything. I would say the way I glitched these photos would be like plucking a petal off of a flower and placing it somewhere else whether that be on to a new flower, on to the stem of the flower, or simply throwing it away. Glitch art is another way to manipulate something to create a new piece, and by glitching these photos, I created something new.

Meme Art: Untitled

My work is titled "Untitled", it is a visual mashup of various components including a digitally manipulated image of Mark Zuckerberg, shown to be wearing a SJSU hat in front of Tower Hall with Sammy in the background faded into the sky. I named it Untitled as memes often take a life of their own, away from their original message, and are often named by the internet realm. I have left it that way for it to be named by others as they have their own take on it.
I decided to use the Mark Zuckerberg image that I had mentioned in class as part of me meme as I thought I could make something interesting with it. Using images I found on the internet via the search engine Bing, I created Untitled. There are several known components found in this image. Firstly, the format of white block at the top of the image with black text is a common meme format. The Mark Zuckerberg image is a common image of Mark Zuckerberg that is shared across various social media platforms especially due to the bizarre facial expression in contrast to the professional and bland persona Zuckerberg is known for. Lastly, there are several components representing SJSU including Sammy, Tower Hall, and the SJSU hat. All of these components together create a new meaning, with Mark Zuckerberg representing a SJSU student receiving a good letter grade from a SJSU professor. I created this image by quick masking the Mark Zuckerberg image to copy and paste on to the SJSU background. The rest of the other components are transparent images that I copied and pasted on to the piece. For Sammy, I used the color select masking method mentioned in the Photoshop tutorials so that I could put Sammy behind the leaves of the trees easily. In order to distort Mark Zuckerberg's face, I utilized the forward warp and bloat feature found under Filter>Liquify to create the odd smile and the enlarged eyes.
This image creates a statement by acting as a representative of an SJSU student and emphasizing it through bizarreness. Firstly, Mark Zuckerberg, the creator of Facebook and a billionaire, is depicted as a SJSU student, a far cry in terms of socioeconomic status. The odd face that Zuckerberg is shown to be making in the picture from digital manipulation further makes his placement odd. By placing the SJSU hat on top and adding the caption “How it highkenuinely feels when Professor Rao gives you an A” puts Zuckerberg in the context of not just any SJSU student, but the viewer. The term highkenuinely (highkey + genuinely) further creates a sense of bizarreness by using a slang term in context of a letter grade as well as with Zuckerberg, who had been mentioned earlier to be a professional individual. Overall, this piece is valuable as it shows how various meanings formed from the digital space and is spread across the internet can be combined together to create a new and relatable message. For someone looking back on this image in the future, it provides insight into various things such as what letter grades meant to students, shows how Mark Zuckerberg was a prominent figure, and provides context as to what kind of slag was used during this time. Furthermore, this piece allows viewers to relate to an experience, regardless of if they know the professor or go to SJSU as the provided context is enough for viewers to get an understanding of the feeling that is conveyed. Art is made with the intention of communication, and this piece does exactly that; communicating the feeling of getting a good grade.

Time-Based Mashup: The American Dream

I titled my work "The American Dream" as it is a commentary on the false hopes of the American Dream given to people living in the United States. It is a combination of an image of a Coke can, a video of a 1950s "nuclear" family, and audio from a 1950s suburban life.
I first became interested in my topic as the Time-Based mashup reminded me of a study that I had heard of a long time ago about subliminal messages in film. It was roughly along the lines of how a single coke drink put in a frame of a movie every second is enough for the conscious mind to not notice it but the subconscious mind would, leading to people wanting to get a drink during the movie. I thought this was an interesting concept. Coca Cola is something that I see as a representation of American culture in terms of both social culture and capitalism. As a result, I combined the image of the Coke can that I found from the search engine Bing with the image of the “perfect” suburban nuclear family from the 1950s from the youtube video “1950s USA, Nuclear Family, Unpacking Groceries, Americana, 16mm” by Kinolibrary. For those unaware, the nuclear family is the typical American family of a wife, husband, and two kids. In combination with the Coke can, I felt that the two concepts were the representation of American culture. I combined these two using the method in the “working with frames lab”. Then, from the youtube video “Living the American Dream, 1950s Suburban Life” by Kinolibrary, I took the audio talking about the ideal American home by downloading it as an mp4 via the Google Colab shared with us. The ending audio of the clip “new home” is repeated over and over again with glitched images of the coke can shown. I repeated the audio by taking the short clip and duplicating it so it could be played over and over again. I glitched the image of the Coke can using the notepadd ++ method taught in the Data Bending Text Editor assignment. All three pieces of media were removed from their original meanings of advertisement and the “Happy American Family” to create a new statement regarding the falsities of the “American Dream”. To understand the overall theme and meaning of the piece, the audience would require some background knowledge on what the “American Dream” is and the prominence of American capitalism and the role it has on American culture.
The new meaning created from my piece is a commentary on the “American Dream” and how capitalism has led to people being unable to achieve this. I thought the study that I mentioned earlier was a great example of how capitalism is ingrained into our lives, and how corporations will do anything, even if it means subtle and unethical advertisement, to lure people into spending their money. Coke is a great example of both American culture and how capitalism has led to the growth of such a company. Coke is always advertised to the basic American or the everyday person. At the end, the phrase “new home” is repeated while becoming louder as clips of a glitched coke can appear, similarly to how ARGs (alternate-reality-games) will often do this to create this idea of something being “broken”. The increasingly louder audio, sudden cut, and repetition create a sense of unease, and also highlights the hypocrisy of the capitalistic society we live in. Capitalism promotes this idea that anyone can achieve the American dream, but preys on the people by trying to get people to constantly purchase goods. I repeated the phrase “new home” as I felt that capitalism has led people to be unable to achieve the simple American dream of owning a home. Owning a home now is becoming harder and harder to do, while purchasing a simple coke is anything we can all do. Younger generations are often told to stop spending money on little things like avocado toast and coffee to save money for a home, when in reality that money, even if saved, would not be enough to buy a home. The glitched coke and “glitched” audio highlights this broken system of capitalism that constantly pushes us to buy, but also criticizes us for not saving for our future. Overall, my work is important because it creates a visual commentary on the American Dream and the system that is supposed to support it. While it requires some background knowledge, it creates a visual representation of the failures of the capitalist system.
